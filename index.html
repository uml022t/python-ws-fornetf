01 / INTRODUCTION

The Silicon Foundation of Artificial Intelligence

02 / HISTORICAL DEVELOPMENT

From General-Purpose Computing to AI Acceleration

The development of AI chips is closely connected to advances in semiconductor manufacturing, parallel computing, deep learning, and high-bandwidth memory.

1960s–1980s | The Early Foundations

Transistors and Microprocessors

Integrated circuits and general-purpose microprocessors established the foundation of modern computing. CPUs became the central processing units of computers.

1990s–2000s | The Rise of GPUs

Parallel Computing Emerges

Graphics processing units evolved to handle parallel graphics workloads. Their parallel architecture later proved valuable for scientific computing and neural networks.

2012 | Deep Learning Breakthrough

GPU-Accelerated Neural Networks

AlexNet demonstrated the power of GPU-accelerated deep learning in the ImageNet competition, helping trigger a major shift in AI research.

2016 | Dedicated AI Accelerators

Google TPU

Google introduced its Tensor Processing Unit, demonstrating the advantages of purpose-built hardware for neural network workloads.

2017–2022 | Transformer Revolution

AI Computing at Scale

The transformer architecture and increasingly large language models created unprecedented demand for compute, memory bandwidth, and interconnect technologies.

2023–2026 | Generative AI Era

Advanced Accelerators and Custom Silicon

Advanced GPUs, custom ASICs, chiplet architectures, HBM memory, and rack-scale computing are transforming AI infrastructure.

03 / CHIP ARCHITECTURE

Understanding Different AI Chip Architectures

Different processors are optimized for different workloads. No single architecture is ideal for every AI application.

CPU — Central Processing Unit

Designed for general-purpose computing, complex control logic, and sequential tasks. CPUs coordinate system operations and support AI workloads.

FlexibleGeneral Purpose

GPU — Graphics Processing Unit

Contains many parallel computing cores and is widely used for training and inference of large neural networks.

Parallel ComputingHigh Throughput

ASIC — Application-Specific IC

Custom-designed chips optimized for specific workloads. They can provide excellent efficiency and predictable performance.

SpecializedEfficient

TPU — Tensor Processing Unit

A specialized AI accelerator designed to efficiently execute tensor and matrix operations in machine learning workloads.

Tensor Operations

NPU — Neural Processing Unit

Designed to accelerate neural network operations, especially low-power inference in smartphones, PCs, and edge devices.

Low PowerEdge AI

FPGA — Field Programmable Gate Array

Reconfigurable hardware that can be customized after manufacturing for flexible acceleration and specialized processing.

Reconfigurable

07 / FUTURE TECHNOLOGY

What Comes Next for AI Chips?

As AI models become larger and more sophisticated, chip innovation is moving beyond traditional transistor scaling toward system-level integration and new computing architectures.

01. Chiplet Architecture

Multiple smaller chiplets can be integrated into a single package, improving design flexibility, yield, and scalability.

02. High-Bandwidth Memory

HBM provides high memory bandwidth to feed increasingly powerful AI processors and reduce data movement bottlenecks.

03. Advanced Packaging

2.5D and 3D integration connect processors and memory at increasingly dense scales.

04. Optical Interconnects

Silicon photonics and optical communication could improve bandwidth and energy efficiency between computing systems.

05. Edge AI

Smaller and more efficient processors will bring AI capabilities to industrial sensors, consumer electronics, and autonomous machines.

06. Neuromorphic Computing

Brain-inspired architectures explore new ways of processing information with potentially lower energy consumption.
